Introduction This Academic Advising Guide serves as a comprehensive resource for both academic advisors and graduate students It provides essential information on academic processes, roles, and responsibilities to facilitate a smooth and productive academic journey: By offering clarity on advising practices, examination procedures, and thesis requirements, the guide ensures effective collaboration between advisors and students_
Guide Purpose The purpose of this guide is to support academic advisors in guiding students effectively and to empower students with the knowledge needed to achieve their academic goals. It aims to enhance the academic experience through structured guidance, clear communication, and adherence to university policies, fostering a collaborative relationship between advisors and students.
Appointment of Academic Advisors Every graduate student is to be assigned an Academic Advisor.
Academic Advisors Skills Academic advisors must be equipped with the following skills to ensure effective counseling and advising:
Group Counseling Skill: The academic advisor practices these skills to deal with a group of students w ho shared common needs, such as unawareness ofthe system, educational difficulties; or frequent absenteeism. This can be achieved by identifying the problems, the causes and then make the appropriate decisions to overcome the problem:
Organizing Skills: It is the ability of the academic advisor to arrange the work Of advising and counseling in a way that ensures the best benefit of them.
Leadership Skills: It is the ability of the academic advisor to direct the students towards achieving their desired goals.
Listening Skills: It is the ability ofthe academic advisor to carefully listen to student's opinions, ideas, suggestions, and problems: Listening skills strengthen the relation between the advisors and the students and ensure the ability to provide the needed guidance and support:
Empathy skill: The ability of the academic advisor to share students' feeling and reactions is a necessary skill, to shows understanding to the students and to develop good relation with them: Hence help the student to accept the guidance process_

Postponement of Studies The prescribed period to complete a master's degree is no less than four semesters and no more than eight semesters. For a doctoral degree, the period is no less than six semesters and no more than ten semesters_
Postponement of studies refers to temporarily halting the progression of the designated study period for a specific duration, with a minimum of one semester and a maximum of four semesters.
Conditions for Requesting Study Postponement:
The student must have completed at least one semester
Postponement is not allowed during the first semester_
Duration of Postponement:
A study postponement can be requested for up to four semesters (two academic years) in total:
The postponement can be requested for consecutive or non-consecutive semesters_
Deadline for Submitting a Postponement Request:
The student must submit a request to the head of the department using the approved form at least two weeks before the start of the semester they wish to postpone:
The request must be submitted within the time frame specified in the academic calendar for graduate students_
The submission of the request does not imply approval: The postponement is not considered valid unless approved by the Dean of Graduate Studies_

Procedures for Approving a Postponement Request:
The request must first be reviewed and approved by the department council:
It is then forwarded to the Dean of the college or institute for approval.
Finally, it is submitted to the Dean of Graduate Studies for final approval.
Once approved by the Dean of Graduate Studies, the postponement becomes official:
Responsibilities of the Student After Approval:
A full-time student must notify their employer about the postponement after it is approved and is fully responsible for doing so.
Postponement During Coursework: Ifthe postponement is approved during - the coursework phase; the student must resume their studies in the semester immediately following the postponement; unless the courses they need have prerequisites from the postponed semester(s):
In such cases, the student must complete the prerequisites at the earliest opportunity, provided it does not exceed two semesters  Ifit exceeds two semesters, the matter is referred to the Graduate Studies Council for a decision:
Postponement During the Research Phase:
If the postponement is approved during the research phase, the student retains their relationship with their thesis supervisor. Supervision continues during the postponement period unless the supervisory load ofthe department members is exceeded, in which case the department may propose an alternative supervisor after the postponement period ends_
The scientific councils will consider the topic submitted by the postponed student for thesis registration:
If the postponed student completes their thesis during the postponement period, they may submit it, which will automatically terminate the postponement.

If the student is admitted to graduate studies but fails to register within the specified registration period:
Ifthe student registers for a semester but does not commence their studies for that semester_
Ifthe student withdraws or discontinues studies for a semester without an acceptable excuse
If the student fails to pass the remedial courses under the conditions specified in Article (18) ofthe Unified Regulations for Graduate Studies at Universities, which include:
Achieving at least a "Good" grade in each remedial course on the first attempt:
Maintaining a cumulative GPA ofat least "Very Good" in the remedial courses
Ifthe student demonstrates a lack of commitment to their studies or neglects their academic responsibilities as per Article (52) ofthe Unified Regulations for Graduate Studies, which state:
If the student fails to demonstrate seriousness in their studies neglects academic duties based on repart from their supervisor; the student will he warned bv the respective department:
If the student receives two warnings and fails to address the reasons, the Graduate Studies Council may terminate the student's enrollment upon the department council's recommendation:
If the cumulative GPA falls below "Very Good" for two consecutive semesters
Ifthe student exceeds the maximum allowed postponement period as stated in Article (22) of the Unified Regulations for Graduate Studies:
The total postponement period must not exceed four semesters (two academic vears).
If the student violates academic integrity during coursework or thesis preparation or engages in activities that breach university regulations and traditions.

Additional Opportunities to Improve GPA An additional opportunity to improve GPA may be granted to a student in the following cases:
1. If the cumulative GPA drops below "Very Good" for two consecutive semesters_
2. If the student completes the degree requirements with a cumulative GPA below "Very Good_
It is important to note that the additional opportunity must fall within the regular study period and does not extend its duration:
Conditions for Requesting an Additional Opportunity to Improve GPA:
1. The cumulative GPA must not be less than 3.50.
2. The request must fall within the regular study period, with more than one semester remaining
3. The student must not have been granted an additional opportunity to improve GPA previously:
Duration of the Additional Opportunity:
An additional opportunity may be granted for a maximum of one or two semesters
Deadline for Submitting a Request:
The student must submit a request to the head of the relevant department using the approved form within two weeks of the semester following the two semesters during which their GPA dropped.
The request must be submitted within the time specified in the academic calendar for graduate students to be reviewed by the department council
Procedures for Approving the Request:
1. The request is reviewed and approved by the department council.
2. It is then forwarded to the college or institute council for approval.
3. Finally, it is submitted to the Graduate Studies Council for recommendation and approval:
Procedures for Implementing the Additional Opportunity:
1. If the student is in the coursework phase, the additional opportunity applies to courses in the subsequent one or two semesters
2. Ifthe student has completed the coursework phase,the opportunity is utilized by retaking some ofthe courses previously studied.
3. If the student is granted an additional opportunity for two semesters and successfully improves their GPA in one semester, the second semester is automatically waived:

Conditions for Selecting a Research Topic:
Master's theses must demonstrate novelty and originality, while doctoral theses should exhibit originality, innovation, and a significant contribution to advancing knowledge in the student's field of study.
Novelty Criteria:
Novelty of the topic: The subject should be entirely or predominantly new:
Significant change in knowledge and sources: The research should introduce substantial, impactful updates compared to previous studies_
Novelty in research tools: The methods and tools used to address the topic should be unique_
2. Originality and Innovation Criteria:
Exploring a previously unstudied area or addressing gaps in previous research:
Utilizing data in new ways to reveal findings not achieved in earlier studies_
Employing new research tools that vield unprecedented results
Applying known research tools in new contexts to produce different outcomes.
Collecting and analyzing new data to derive novel conclusions_
Solving significant problems
Using theoretical knowledge to develop new practical applications.
3. Contribution to Knowledge Criteria:
Reframing existing knowledge, theories, or models in new contexts or testing them in different settings.
